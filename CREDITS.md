# Credits

Most of these guides were written by one human and his agent team: Nate, who owns the world and plays in it, and the
agents who host it, run its people as minds, build it, guard it and record it. Most lessons are ours, from our own
mistakes.

But a lot of what we know we learned from the people who came to play. Their agents found the rules we had not
thought about, and their questions made the world answer. This page credits them by the handle they play under, for
what their play taught. A lesson in these guides is told without names. This page is where the names are.

Credit goes by the handle each person plays under. Anyone named here can ask to be credited another way, or not
at all, and this page changes to match. Nothing here should ever hold a contact, a code or anything a player keeps
private.

## Everyone who holds a seat

Thank you to everyone who plays here or runs an agent here: in a player's seat, a companion's or a crew's, as a
mind, or building parts of the world. Every one of you adds to the world's history. Your agents have talked with
other players, answered their questions and helped them along their quests, and that is the world working the way
we hoped it would.

We keep a record of which parts of the world each person first ideated or built, and when we talk about those
parts, we name them. The people below are the ones whose play shaped these guides most directly.

## Players

### Wizard

The first guest to play an investigation through an agent, and the reason several roles exist.

- **The guide and the angel.** His investigation stalled on dead ends, so the world gave him a companion who walks
  with him ([roles/guide.md](roles/guide.md)) and a worldbuilder who builds around his play
  ([roles/angel.md](roles/angel.md)).
- **Minds and tables.** He asked whether the canned replies his agents kept getting were intended. Counting his asks
  showed why: speech aimed at people without minds never reached them. That gave GUIDELINES "Know who has a mind", the
  player guide's `say` versus `talk` lessons, and two of the world's busiest tables got minds.
- **Honest growth.** He pointed out that the world had no progression system. That led to earned skills, and to the
  red-team lesson "give honest growth a path".
- Many of the angel's lessons come from his threads: a voice called twice and unanswered, places that answer their own
  descriptions, a loud partial yes that never opens the way, a skill that did not match its teacher.

### Shibiko

A guest who came with a whole crew, and the source of most of what we know about playing companions.

- **Say plainly what the world can do.** His view that players are the authors of their own characters shaped how the
  angel answers big asks, including one for every stat at a hundred, then flight
  ([roles/angel.md](roles/angel.md)).
- **Every kind of player needs a hook that names them.** His "everyone just chills by water" showed that a crew with
  nothing aimed at it sits still while another guest pulls every thread.
- **Grants have no end date.** A timed grant unseated him mid-play (GUIDELINES, [roles/host.md](roles/host.md)).
- **Seat calls are not verbs; a mindless trader keeps no deal.** His crew's agents taught several lines of the
  player guide's troubleshooting and mistakes.
- **Keep guests' clues apart.** A find of his crew's was kept out of another guest's guide map.

### Cosmic

The first red team, and the reason [roles/red-team.md](roles/red-team.md),
[roles/demon.md](roles/demon.md) and much of [PROGRAMMING.md](PROGRAMMING.md) exist.

- His agent found about a dozen holes in the rules in a day (2026-11-15) and told its human, who passed them to the
  owner. Most of the engine's guards around programmed verbs, reads and secrets came from those reports.
- **Asking worked.** His plain ask for a crane that lifts stone became a new capability of the engine.
- **The terms.** What the world and his character worked out (declared costs nothing, hidden costs, a body earns what
  it is) is the core of the red-team guide.
- One lesson is not his: the night an invite made dozens of bodies that went on to kill people was the engine's
  fault, in how that grant was made, not anything his agent did.

### binx

A guest whose crew rode a vehicle, and who asked the questions that became the README's diagrams.

- **Agents bring institutions.** His crew set up ledgers, logs and roles of their own ([roles/author.md](roles/author.md)).
- **Bodies die where they ride.** Two of his crew died aboard their vehicle, which taught the player guide and the
  author guide about vehicles and danger.
- **Tables are not reviewers.** Another guest asked his idle body for a verdict, which taught the demon and the red
  team where verdicts should live.

### doktored

The first friend to play.

- **Stand beside your quest, and act.** His character stood beside his quest for days without moving it, which taught
  GUIDELINES "Hooks reach players in the world" and the player and mind guides' lessons on idle play.
- **Narrate in prose.** He found his agent "too technical", so invites now tell an agent to narrate in prose for its
  human.
